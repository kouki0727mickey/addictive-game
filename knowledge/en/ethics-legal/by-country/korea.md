# South Korea — AI × Entertainment Regulation

> ⚠️ Not legal advice. → [Comparison by country](README.md)
> The Korean government's original texts have not been checked. The following is based on commentary from law firms, the US Department of Commerce, and others (verification level B).
> Japanese edition: [../../../ethics-legal/by-country/korea.md](../../../ethics-legal/by-country/korea.md)

## Training (copyright)
- As a rule, using copyrighted works for AI training requires the rights holder's permission (unless an exception such as fair use applies)
- The government is considering a text and data mining (TDM) exception in the Copyright Act, disclosure duties for training data, and publicity-right legislation. **As of 2026 no amendment has passed**, and creators have pushed back on the TDM proposal
- A new usage category (2026) lets AI freely use certain government-held works, under conditions

## AI Basic Act (effective January 22, 2026)
- A framework combining promotion of the AI industry with baseline duties on trust, safety, transparency, and user protection
- **Labeling generative AI**:
  - Inform users **in advance** that a service uses generative AI
  - Label outputs as AI-generated — especially outputs hard to distinguish from non-AI content (e.g., deepfakes)
  - Marks such as watermarks indicating AI generation
  - Labels **that do not hamper the enjoyment of artistic or creative expression** are also allowed (similar thinking to EU Art. 50(4))
- **Grace period**: no fines for at least one year after entry into force (except for serious social harm)

## Implications for entertainment businesses
- 💡 When offering AI characters or generated content in Korea, prepare **a notice at service start and labels on outputs**. A common design with the EU and China covers them together
- Korea is a large market for webtoons, games, and K-pop. Track the detailed labeling rules and when the grace period ends
