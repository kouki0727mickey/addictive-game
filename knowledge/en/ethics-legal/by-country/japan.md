# Japan — AI × Entertainment Regulation

> ⚠️ Not legal advice. → [Comparison by country](README.md) / [Cross-cutting overview (Japanese)](../../../ethics-legal/ethics-legal.md)
> Japanese edition: [../../../ethics-legal/by-country/japan.md](../../../ethics-legal/by-country/japan.md)

## Training
- **Copyright Act Article 30-4**: uses not aimed at "enjoying" the thoughts or sentiments expressed in a work, such as data analysis, are permitted without permission under certain conditions
- Exception: where it would "unreasonably prejudice the interests of the copyright owner"
- Agency for Cultural Affairs, "General Understanding on AI and Copyright" (2024): additional training intended to deliberately imitate or output a specific creator's works may fall outside the provision, because an "enjoyment" purpose coexists

## Outputs
- Infringement is possible where an output is **similar** to an existing work and **relied on** it (the usual test)
- Without human creative contribution, AI outputs are unlikely to be recognized as copyrighted works
- **Example**: In October 2025, after large numbers of videos resembling Japanese anime were made with the video generator Sora 2, the Japanese government asked OpenAI not to infringe copyrights, and CODA sent a written request asking, among other things, that it not train without permission (→ [case A05](../../domains/case-studies.md))

## Voice & likeness (right of publicity)
On August 7, 2026, a Ministry of Justice study group published its interpretive guideline on right-of-publicity infringement by generative AI.

- **Voice**, like likeness, is covered by the right of publicity and the right not to be used without good reason
- Test: cases "aimed solely at exploiting customer-attracting power," such as the three categories from the Supreme Court's Pink Lady decision: (1) making the voice itself a product to be enjoyed, (2) attaching it to differentiate a product, (3) using it in advertising
- **Even a similar voice** counts as "use of the person's voice" if, considering the degree of similarity and information that brings the person to mind, customers can identify it as that person's voice
- Examples of infringement: voice messages, voice stickers, audiobooks, **AI covers**, narration, alarm clocks, car navigation, smart speakers, advertising
- **Voice actors**: it is enough that the voice itself has power to drive sales; the name or face need not be widely known
- **Character voices that are not a real person's voice** are out of scope
- Human impressions and voice mimicry do not infringe unless they cause confusion with the person
- Intellectual Property Strategic Program 2026: consideration continues, including amending the Unfair Competition Prevention Act

## User protection
- Gacha: the Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations ("complete gacha" ban) and industry self-regulation
- AI companions and AI labeling: no dedicated legal duties as of September 2026
- Personal data: Act on the Protection of Personal Information (conversation logs, memory data)

## Implications for entertainment businesses
- 💡 Do not rely on "Japan is lenient on training" alone. Rights holders, the government, and the guideline are all becoming stricter on **outputs resembling specific works** and on **voice**
- For AI voices, use either "a licensed real voice" or "a voice that does not imitate a real person" (→ [ai-characters P38](../../ai-characters/patterns.md))
